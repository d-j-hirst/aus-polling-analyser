# C++ turnout shadow calculation

This document explains how to reproduce and inspect the C++ calculation of votes remaining during a live count. It describes the implemented input format, the comparison with Python and the diagnostic output produced alongside a LiveV2 run.

The calculation starts from a fixed set of possible pre-election vote counts and updates them using the current first-preference counts. A **shadow calculation** runs alongside the existing forecast and records its estimates for comparison. It does not change the forecast's vote totals, uncertainty or winner probabilities. The exporter currently supports SA 2026 and Federal 2025.

## What the calculation uses

The **frozen prior** is the pre-election distribution: a set of possible final formal vote totals and category counts, generated by the Python turnout prior. An outcome contains counts for every district together, retaining relationships between districts. The C++ updater reads these outcomes rather than fitting historical parameters itself. Each update starts from this same account; it does not update the result of an earlier snapshot.

An input unit is an ordinary booth, an ordinary pre-poll voting centre (PPVC), or a declaration category such as Postal. Its metadata identifies its district, historical group, starting weight, reliable historical match, EAV service status and any known closure or calibration exclusion. The weights divide the historical groups; they do not provide evidence for historical category splits that were never reported.

Reported ordinary and PPVC booths are approximately complete. Declaration counts are lower bounds. Counted votes remain unchanged, while estimated additions are allocated within a finite enrolment account using the same transformed calculations as Python. EAV services receive their separate starting-size treatment. Conservative PPVC compensation and the explicitly configured delayed-return adjustment follow the Python replay rules. Experimental pooled size-change alternatives are not enabled by this contract.

Earlier cumulative declaration counts and whole historical-group totals supply evidence about whether counting has slowed. A historical group is a division supported by the earlier results: SA's early group includes PPVC booths and early declarations, whereas federal data supports separate categories. The artifact records each group's exact observed sum under `allocation:<group>`; Postal uses its existing category total. These measurements include ordinary and PPVC booths where they belong to the group, rather than trying to reconstruct their split from a combined ordinary-vote total.

The calculation uses the feed's timestamp, not the filename's capture time. Archived observations after the current source time are excluded, and the current LiveV2 counts replace any observation at that time. A missing measurement remains absent; it is not recorded as zero. Without earlier observations, the slowing evidence is zero and the broad prediction is retained.

For Federal 2025, Postal has the explicitly supplied receipt deadline of 16 May at 6 pm. The deadline gradually discounts evidence from a pause while postal returns can still arrive; it never closes a category. Other elections do not inherit this date. The ECSA first-preference finalisation flag can close a district account. Declared winners and two-candidate completion are not used as first-preference finalisation flags.

## Why remaining allowances can recede

An aggregate allowance can assign too many votes to the small declaration categories still open after the larger booths have finished. The updater retains each declaration's own expected remainder before that allowance rescaled it. As counting in the whole group settles, it moves gradually towards the own-category estimate. It gives less influence to an estimate that would increase the allowance: a larger prior alone is weak evidence that more votes are still to arrive.

The progress response is `1 - exp(-(evidence / 0.5)^2)`. Within each prior outcome, a logistic response with scale 0.5 in the difference between the two log odds favours reductions smoothly. These scales are explicit assumptions from the offline comparison, rather than fitted completion probabilities. There are no completion cutoffs or hard vote ceilings. Joint transformed accounting allows a lower formal total while preserving counted votes, booth estimates and the enrolment bound. The category-specific late-batch mixture is applied afterwards, with its usual evidence and receipt-window treatment.

The allocation model is identified as `live-allocation-1`, with count contract `live-counts-6`. Older artifacts lack its required group history and must be re-exported; no historical refit is needed. The count-version change also makes older GUI builds reject the new artifact instead of silently using their previous allocation rule. The adopted shadow rule uses the existing category uncertainty. The optional extra spread for unsupported finer splits remains an offline sensitivity experiment.

## Why late batches are retained separately

A single standard deviation would obscure the distinction between an almost finished count and a small probability of a substantial late batch. The updater therefore retains three components: the broad previous-expectation distribution, very small additions, and a wider late-batch distribution. Their weights change continuously with counting evidence.

There is also an explicit, shared outcome in which the entire district receives no further votes. Its probability grows smoothly with count-weighted slowing evidence and falls with the broad expected remainder, including unreported booths. A known postal receipt window discounts it. It transfers probability from the broad and small-addition branches while preserving each category's unconditional late-batch probability. These are exploratory shared settings, not fitted probabilities of completion; counting can resume in a later snapshot.

The sample matrices describe the branch with additional votes. The result retains the no-addition probability separately, and reported means and intervals combine both branches. For example, a 60% probability of no further votes and a conditional expectation of 100 additional votes gives a mean of 40. Keeping this outcome explicitly avoids dependence on whether a small preparation sample happened to draw it. Authoritative finalisation makes no further votes certain.

Each prior preparation outcome supplies eight inexpensive count draws by default. Both additional components are also evaluated deliberately, even when a mixed sample does not select the rare batch. The in-memory result retains each component's transformed location, available capacity and weight for every preparation outcome. These parameters are suitable inputs to a separate party-share preparation; they have not yet been installed in the main forecast sampler.

The artifact also retains distinct prior count responses for turnout, formality, early-vote conversion, postal conversion and composition within historical groups. A response describes how category counts change with one uncertainty source. These are prior count responses, not current party-share sensitivities. The shadow output does not combine them with LiveV2's existing party-bias sensitivities or treat them as additional independent forecast errors.

## Reproducing the input and comparisons

Run the exporter from `analysis/`, using the existing prior fixture, no-results allocation exports and retained source archives described in the Python replay reports:

```powershell
.\.venv-win\Scripts\python.exe -B -m scripts.turnout.turnout_cpp_fixture
```

Use `--election 2026sa` or `--election 2025fed` to export one election. `--archive` and `--downloads` select the source directories. The exporter performs no historical fitting. Its default is 256 preparation outcomes with eight count draws per outcome.

Re-export after updating the shadow code to `live-allocation-1`, then rebuild the application before using that artifact. The loader rejects artifacts without the matching allocation-model identifier.

The ignored `downloads/turnout/cpp-shadow/` directory receives two files per election:

- `2026sa-shadow.json` or `2025fed-shadow.json`: frozen prior outcomes, static unit metadata, source history, version identifiers, source hashes and separate prior count responses.
- `2026sa-checkpoint.json` or `2025fed-checkpoint.json`: the latest retained complete-election input account, plus Python mean totals and unit estimates for a portable C++ comparison and timing check.

The committed files under `tests/fixtures/turnout/` contain smaller self-contained comparisons: six SA districts and eight federal electorates, each retaining eight original prior outcomes. Each election has a frozen no-results case and four source checkpoints. The comparison population is explicit: shared progress measurements are recomputed within these selected districts, so these small fixtures are not substitutes for the full-election replay scores.

The portable `TurnoutModelTests` compares broad totals, category counts, unit additions, progress evidence, mixed outcomes and the parameters of both conditional late-count distributions. It also checks explicit finalisation and rejects incompatible versions, invalid counts and unsupported clocks. It runs through the existing portable test scripts. After building the portable tests, a complete-election checkpoint can be checked from the repository root with:

```powershell
.\cli-build\tests\TurnoutModelTests.exe . downloads\turnout\cpp-shadow\2026sa-shadow.json downloads\turnout\cpp-shadow\2026sa-checkpoint.json
.\cli-build\tests\TurnoutModelTests.exe . downloads\turnout\cpp-shadow\2025fed-shadow.json downloads\turnout\cpp-shadow\2025fed-checkpoint.json
```

The fixtures and complete-election checkpoints use the same deterministic quantile grid in Python and C++. This separates differences in the calculation from differences between random generators. Ordinary Python replay scoring retains its existing generator.

## Enabling LiveV2 diagnostics

Set the artifact path before starting the GUI application from the same PowerShell session:

```powershell
$env:POLLING_ANALYSER_TURNOUT_SHADOW = (Resolve-Path downloads\turnout\cpp-shadow\2026sa-shadow.json).Path
```

The application must be rebuilt with the new sources. The setting is read when LiveV2 constructs the election, and the artifact's election must match the run. An application already running does not inherit a later environment-variable change. Remove the setting to disable the shadow calculation:

```powershell
Remove-Item Env:POLLING_ANALYSER_TURNOUT_SHADOW
```

LiveV2 matches units by district, name and role, attaches its mapped first-preference counts and prepares the shadow account once. Scenario copies share that immutable result instead of repeating the count preparation or copying all outcomes. A missing expected unit, changed role, new counted unit, conflicting enrolment or invalid artifact stops the enabled shadow run with an explanation; it does not silently discard votes.

The existing `.analysis.json` diagnostic export gains an optional `turnout_shadow` entry. It contains district totals and 95% count intervals, the probability of no further votes, counted and expected category totals, unit estimates, PPVC compensation shifts, slowing evidence, component probabilities and conditional means, source identity, provenance and elapsed time. **Mean remaining** is mean estimated final formal votes minus currently counted formal votes. A conditional batch mean answers what that category could add if the batch component applied while other counts were held fixed; it is not the ordinary expected addition. Component probabilities include the shared no-addition event and sum to one for each category.

Each unit additionally reports its own expected remainder before balancing, the imposed remainder before release, and the remainder after release but before the late-batch mixture. These values explain whether the aggregate allowance inflated a small category and how much the new rule relaxed it. `allocation_group_evidence` is the whole-group slowing score, while `allocation_progress_weight` is its response before the per-outcome directional adjustment. `broad_mean_total` now describes the account after allocation release and before the late-batch mixture. The diagnostic includes the allocation-model identifier and both response scales.

The FP comparison preserves counted party votes and applies the existing LiveV2 remaining-vote composition to the changed remaining amount. If the existing projection supplies no additions, its overall projected composition is used. Unsupported compositions are identified rather than invented. The reported percentage-point changes illustrate count effects with composition held fixed; they are not a new forecast distribution or evidence that winner uncertainty is calibrated.

## Comparison results and limits

The purpose of the parity checks is to establish that C++ implements the Python updater, rather than to establish that either model's assumptions are correct. The ten small comparison cases and the latest complete-election mean comparisons pass with a maximum difference of approximately `1.7e-9` votes. Explicit finalisation preserves the exact counted account.

The timing check asks how much count-only preparation adds at the intended reduced sample size. With 256 prior outcomes and eight count draws per outcome, a controlled WSL comparison of the former and directional allocation paths took 3.67 and 3.62 seconds for SA, and 10.65 and 10.26 seconds for Federal. Another CPU-intensive task was running, so these absolute times describe a loaded machine and the small differences do not establish a speed improvement. The paired runs show no material added cost from the allocation step in these checkpoints. They exclude party-share preparation and main simulation iterations and do not establish compliance with the full application time budget.

The portable comparisons pass under WSL with GCC 9 using the repository's existing calendar helpers. The count component can be compiled as C++17 independently; the existing application and portable pipeline build scripts retain their C++20 setting.

The SA inputs here use the raw live feed. They exclude the retrospective Croydon/Taylor relabellings identified using reviewed final results, and retain the raw zero entries that were later reviewed as missing category data. Consequently these diagnostics must not be compared directly with the repaired-data scores as though the inputs were identical. Existing explicit source-reader corrections, including Narungga, remain in place. Final results are not inputs to the C++ count update.

Historical observations are supplied in the artifact; this bridge does not maintain a persistent history of new GUI runs. Regenerate the artifact to include additional retained source observations, keeping the frozen prior options unchanged. Hashes identify the source revisions used during export. Changed source evidence or a changed prior sampler requires regeneration rather than silently combining new evidence with an old fitted fixture.

The changed GUI-facing translation units pass MSVC syntax checks. This revision has not been verified by a complete GUI rebuild and replay or by measuring party/winner outputs and total application runtime. The shadow account leaves the existing normal party-share variation in place; explicit late-batch party-share sampling is not yet part of the live forecast.

## Offline comparison of remaining-vote allocation

This comparison examines whether an aggregate vote allowance assigns too many votes to the categories that remain open. It also checks the opposite risk: removing that allowance may underestimate votes that have genuinely not been counted yet. The experiment uses the same frozen priors, counted votes, source history and final scoring data as the existing Python replay. It does not change the Python live-model defaults, C++ shadow calculation or exported GUI artifacts.

Run from `analysis/`:

```powershell
.\.venv-win\Scripts\python.exe -B -m scripts.turnout.turnout_allocation_experiment --elections 2026sa 2025fed
```

Here, a group is a category supported by the historical data: SA's early group combines PPVC and early declaration votes, while its remaining group combines ordinary booths and other declarations. Federal data supports finer historical groups. For reproducibility, the experiment's `current` label retains the original treatment before directional release was adopted: it exhausts the aggregate allowance. Immediate release instead uses each declaration category's estimate before that balancing step. Gradual release moves from the original allocation towards those estimates as observed whole-group counting settles. Its response is `1 - exp(-(evidence / scale)^2)`; the comparison uses scales 0.3 and 0.5, with the larger value retaining aggregate influence longer. With no progress evidence, gradual release retains the original prediction exactly. It changes the continuation estimate rather than declaring individual categories complete. The existing category-specific small-addition, exceptional-batch and no-addition treatments are applied afterwards. The published federal postal receipt window discounts both category-specific and aggregate progress evidence.

The comparison additionally assumes an extra standard deviation of 0.5 in log odds for finer category shares where the historical data supports only an aggregate group. For a small share, the added variation alone spans approximately a factor of 2.7 either side of its starting value over a 95% range, before counted votes are applied. This is a sensitivity check, not a fitted uncertainty estimate. Historical federal category priors are not widened by this assumption. Each category is analysed as a subset of formal votes, and declarations jointly share unused enrolment capacity; observed counts and booth estimates remain unchanged.

The directional comparison asks whether release should give less influence to an individual category estimate that is larger than the aggregate-balanced allowance. That larger prior estimate, by itself, is weak evidence that more votes remain. With `--experiment directional`, the script compares the current treatment, gradual release at scale 0.5, and a directional version with and without the additional uncertainty assumption. The directional version varies smoothly between stronger downward influence and weaker upward influence. Its logistic response uses a scale of 0.5 in the difference between the two log odds; this is an exploratory assumption, not a fitted parameter or a hard ceiling on votes. Joint accounting can still change several categories together. The progress weight recorded in the output is the whole-group response before this additional per-outcome adjustment.

Use `--dates` to evaluate selected dates while retaining their preceding history. The federal comparison can reuse the exact category history in an existing shadow artifact, checking its source clocks and hashes and still reading each evaluated snapshot's booth results in full:

```powershell
.\.venv-win\Scripts\python.exe -B -m scripts.turnout.turnout_allocation_experiment --elections 2026sa --experiment directional
.\.venv-win\Scripts\python.exe -B -m scripts.turnout.turnout_allocation_experiment --elections 2025fed --experiment directional --dates 2025-05-07 2025-05-20 2025-05-31 --history-artifact ../downloads/turnout/cpp-shadow/2025fed-shadow.json
```

Outputs are saved in the ignored `downloads/turnout/allocation-experiment/` directory, one comparison JSON per election. They define the alternatives and score columns, list the evaluated timestamps and retained history, and record exact source and code hashes. SA uses all retained replay checkpoints. Federal scores cover election night, intermediate counts, the postal receipt window and final counting; other daily sources still contribute progress history. Mean absolute errors and interval scores are in votes, with equal weight per known district or supported district/group. Positive signed error means overprediction. An upper-bound miss means the reviewed final count exceeds the predicted 95% upper limit. Detailed category records retain their own estimates, imposed allowances, release weights and resulting means. `--dry-run` calculates without saving output; `--samples` and `--count-draws` select the preparation and additional count sample sizes.

Directional outputs use the separate `2026sa-directional.json` and `2025fed-directional.json` names, preserving the original comparison files. Those outputs identify their evaluated checkpoints explicitly; a selected-date comparison does not establish performance at the omitted checkpoints.
