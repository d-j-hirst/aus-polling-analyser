# Using federal booths to estimate One Nation’s vote within Victorian districts

This study tests whether a booth reporting early on election night comes from a relatively strong or weak area for One Nation. It uses the May 2025 federal House results to predict the March 2026 South Australian Assembly count, then applies the tested method to the Victorian roster available on 22 November 2026. The quantity throughout is One Nation’s share of **formal first preferences**.

For a Victorian **ordinary election-day booth**, the SA replay supports pooling the three closest federal ordinary sites and carrying across about **0.72 of the difference in log odds**. The district’s expected vote stays fixed. For a **named early voting centre**, the SA evidence favours retaining the district’s early-vote expectation. These are research results for Australian Election Forecasts. [Victorian district illustrations](vic-districts.csv)

## What did the first SA booths tell us about the rest?

The test uses the first archived ECSA update in each district with at least one ordinary booth reporting 100 formal votes, then the first update with at least three such booths. It includes every positive return visible at that time, including smaller counts and simultaneous returns. The target is the final combined share at the ordinary booths still on zero. A uniform estimate gives those booths the share seen so far. A geographic estimate allows for the locations that supplied it.

Each table entry is mean absolute error in percentage points. Each of the 47 districts has equal weight. The first column tests the geographic pattern at individual booths, giving each method the correct overall ordinary share and weighting booths by their formal votes. The other two columns test election-night predictions using the returns and size estimates available at the time.

| Federal geography used | Individual ordinary booth | Remaining booths after first substantial return | Remaining booths after three substantial returns |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Uniform within the district | 2.89 | 3.26 | 1.78 |
| Nearest ordinary site | **2.25** | 3.43 | 1.35 |
| Three nearest ordinary sites | 2.36 | **2.69** | **1.16** |
| Five nearest ordinary sites | 2.41 | 2.88 | 1.27 |
| Near-coincident site or three sites from the district’s own booth map | 2.32 | 3.48 | 1.46 |
| Three nearest, district average if the closest is over 10 km away | 2.36 | 2.71 | 1.17 |

The single closest federal site gave the smallest average error at individual booths. Pooling three sites gave a better estimate of the outstanding vote. When the choice among six geographic rules and the uniform estimate was made using only other areas’ replays, **all ten held-out federal areas selected three sites**. The 10 km fallback improved on the uniform estimate in 27 of 47 districts at the first milestone and 30 of 47 at the third. Its average gain was 0.55 points at the first milestone, with a 95% geographic resampling interval from −0.32 to 1.46. After three returns the gain was 0.61 points, with an interval from 0.35 to 0.87. Using the final sizes of the outstanding booths as a hindsight check gave errors of 2.64 and 1.18 points. **Heysen** shows what the adjustment can do. The first substantial return came from a neighbourhood where federal results already indicated strong One Nation support. The three-site method estimated **16.9%** at the remaining booths, which finished at **15.5%**. In **Narungga** the adjustment went the wrong way. Two early returns gave One Nation 34.7%, the method estimated 29.9% for the rest, and they finished at 37.9%. [District forecast comparisons](replay-comparisons.csv)

## How much evidence is there for early voting centres?

SA had 59 named early centre records, of which 58 recorded formal votes. Only **11 districts** had two positive centres to compare, accounting for 22 centres and 84,784 formal votes. Ten of those districts also supplied an election-night return suitable for predicting the other centre. Flinders had no qualifying early report in the archive.

| One Nation estimate | Individual early centre | Other early centre after first substantial return |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| District early-vote share | 2.59 | 6.47 |
| Three nearby federal ordinary sites, fitted strength | 3.17 | 8.20 |
| Nearest federal pre-poll site, fitted strength | 2.59 | 6.19 |
| Three nearby federal ordinary sites, fixed half-strength | 2.21 | 5.73 |

The fixed half-strength comparison lowered the average error, but improved only four of the ten election-night predictions. Choosing the method from other areas’ replays gave 7.96 points of error, against 6.47 for a uniform estimate. The early-centre evidence is too thin and inconsistent to assign a separate One Nation geographic adjustment in Victoria. The earlier study addresses a different question about Labor, Coalition and Greens patterns at Victorian EVCs. ## Using the result in Victoria

1. **Start from the reporting district’s expected One Nation share for ordinary votes.** Pool the One Nation and formal votes at the three nearest distinct 2025 federal ordinary sites, including nearby sites across boundaries. Sum ballots before dividing, so a large federal booth contributes more than a small one. Federal returns at the same rounded coordinates are combined into one site.
2. **Carry over part of the local difference.** The full SA estimate is **0.724438** for three sites. Across the ten held-out-area fits it ranged from 0.689 to 0.748. Odds are the ratio of One Nation votes to all other formal votes. If those odds are twice as high in one federal neighbourhood as in another, the fitted state odds are about **1.65 times** as high. Half a ballot on each side keeps zero counts usable. This transfers a geographic pattern across two elections with different overall levels of support. [Fitted strengths](strengths.csv)
3. **Preserve the district forecast.** Adjust all located booth estimates by the same amount on the log-odds scale until their vote-weighted mean gives the supplied ordinary share. The 20% illustration below weights booths by the VEC’s expected ordinary volumes. In the live baseline, use the forecast’s own turnout weights for both setting the booth shares and adding them back together. Where the closest federal site is over 10 km away, use the ordinary district share. The distance limit is a cautious transfer assumption. Only three SA ordinary booths, with 235 formal votes between them, lay beyond it. There are **37 such Victorian booths** with 4,282 expected votes, or 0.27% of the published ordinary volume. The other **1,815 of 1,852** ordinary district/venue records receive geographic estimates. [Live turnout weight comparison](baseline.md#what-does-an-ordinary-baseline-mean-in-practice)
4. **Use actual counts as they arrive.** Compare the returns with the expectation for those particular booths, then apply the inferred level to outstanding votes. In a multi-party forecast the other candidates divide the remaining share, with their district totals reconciled as well. The existing Victorian federal-booth trial sets differences *between* districts. Centering the new pattern within each district preserves that earlier estimate. All 310 Victorian EVC records in the illustration take their own district’s early share. Postal, absent and mobile votes have different catchments and supply no location signal here. [Existing Victorian district trial](../../README.md#victorian-one-nation-district-primary-trial)

One Nation appears in all 88 districts on the 2026 Victorian Assembly candidate roster. The previous Assembly booth history has a One Nation candidate in only five of them. In the 28 SA districts whose predecessor had no 2022 One Nation candidate, the ordinary-booth error fell from **2.43 to 1.93** points with the three-site rule and distance fallback. In the other 19 districts it fell from **3.56 to 2.98**. A follow-up compares federal geography with the old state pattern and a blend of the two. It also applies them at the published November Victorian district medians. [Results by previous candidature](prior-candidate.csv) · [One Nation’s 2026 baseline](baseline.md)

For a concrete example, suppose One Nation is expected to take **20%** of Geelong’s ordinary votes. The three federal sites nearest Fyans Park recorded 272 One Nation votes from 5,135 formal votes. Those nearest Bannockburn recorded 443 from 2,946. The illustration gives **14.3% at Fyans Park** and **27.8% at Bannockburn**, while all 24 Geelong booths still average 20.0% at their VEC volume weights. The 20% is an illustration supplied equally to all districts. A live forecast would supply each district’s own estimates. The SA test measures booth shares and the outstanding ordinary vote. Victorian accuracy, whole-seat forecast volatility, preference flows and winner probabilities require separate evidence. ## Evidence and validation

The outcome comes from ECSA’s reviewed final first-preference returns, data version 912. All 47 districts had a One Nation candidate. The result feed identifies named ordinary and early booths, with a separate count for each reporting district at a shared venue. Six of 688 ordinary records had zero formal votes. The 682 positive booths cast **481,926** formal votes, including **106,699** for One Nation. The state adapter checks the district totals and this study reconciles One Nation’s booth counts to ECSA’s candidate summary, including mobile votes for that source check. Mobile teams and unlocated counting categories are excluded from the geographic tests. [ECSA results review](https://ecsa.sa.gov.au/se2026news/se2026-results-review-complete) · [ECSA polling locations](https://www.ecsa.sa.gov.au/se2026-evc-pp)

ECSA’s results file leaves coordinates empty. All 747 named records joined uniquely to Ben Raue’s 2026 polling-place workbook by district, normalised booth name and type. One workbook entry placed the Parafield Gardens Recreation Centre in Victoria. Its operator confirms the address on Kings and Martins Roads in Adelaide. The study uses the midpoint of its OpenStreetMap building bounds. It is Playford’s sole early centre. The correction affects no within-district comparison. Federal counts and coordinates are from the AEC’s final 2025 House files. The Victorian ordinary counts aggregated over the project’s existing district map reproduce all 88 project district records exactly. [The Tally Room’s SA dataset](https://www.tallyroom.com.au/64837) · [Venue operator](https://gardensrc.com.au/) · [Building outline © OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/475189747) · [AEC House downloads](https://results.aec.gov.au/31496/Website/HouseDownloadsMenu-31496-Csv.htm)

For each geography and voting mode, subtract the district’s own average log odds from the booth log odds in each election. Fit the state differences to the federal differences, giving equal weight to each district and weighting booths inside it by final state formal votes. Predict each district using coefficients fitted without its federal area. An area is the federal division contributing the most ordinary ballots to the state district’s booth map. This holds out neighbouring state districts together. A second test holds out one state district at a time. The tests condition on a known district share only for individual-booth scoring. The replay learns the level from that update’s published votes. Uncertainty intervals resample whole federal areas 4,000 times and describe variation within this one state election. [Scores by validation method](scores.csv)

The replay examined 80 archived polling-night captures. Five early files contained a pre-election zero feed. Source updates before polls closed are excluded. The local observations retain the capture filename and ECSA’s source time. The published district comparisons identify the reporting milestone and give the forecast, eventual outstanding share and error in percentage points. A milestone can contain several booths arriving together. The one-booth milestone included one positive booth in 35 districts, two in nine districts and three in three districts. For prospective size weights, 647 ordinary booths matched a 2022 count by district and name, including Frome’s continuing booths in the renamed Ngadjuri district. The other 41 used the district median of matched booths. Early centres received equal prior weights. Relative sizes in the additional final-size comparison use hindsight only for aggregation and scoring. [ECSA Ngadjuri district profile](https://ecsa.sa.gov.au/electoral-districts/electoral-district-profiles/ngadjuri) · [Source revisions and capture hashes](sources.json)

## Reproducing the study

Run these commands from the repository root. To rerun the historical replay, first prepare the local first-return table with `record.py --feeds <SA capture directory>` and the source files described below. The study then runs offline. The tests check published comparisons and district share calculations on any checkout, and repeat the full replay when the local inputs are present.

~~~powershell
.\analysis\.venv-win\Scripts\python.exe -B analysis\reports\2026vic-one-nation-booths\study.py --historical-only
.\analysis\.venv-win\Scripts\python.exe -B analysis\reports\2026vic-one-nation-booths\test_study.py
~~~

Each row in the historical input represents one district, mode and booth. The columns named after geographic rules contain **federal One Nation ballots / all federal formal ballots**. The unprefixed One Nation and formal counts are the final SA outcomes. Distance columns are kilometres. The six geographic choices are the nearest ordinary site, the three or five closest, a same-site-or-three rule (within 250 metres), the same rule restricted to the reporting district’s mapped ordinary sites, and the nearest federal pre-poll site. In the outputs, `selected` chooses by the fit to training booths and `replay-selected` chooses by the mean error at training election-night milestones. Positive gain means a lower absolute error than the uniform estimate. The `gain_low` and `gain_high` columns contain the geographic resampling interval in points. [Reviewed SA booth inputs](sa-booths.csv) · [Election-night scores](election-night.csv)

With the local Victorian roster available, running the study without `--historical-only` also writes the per-venue illustration under `operator/`. Use `--target-pct` to change the illustrative percentage and `--out` to write a different scenario to its own directory. To reconstruct the input tables, `record.py` uses the retained AEC and ECSA source files, the two public venue workbooks, the prepared VEC roster and candidates, and the SA election-night XML directory supplied with `--feeds`. `--sa-places` and `--vic-venues` accept explicit workbook paths. The first-return observations and full milestone accounts are saved under `operator/count-history/` in the output directory. The source manifest identifies the exact revisions. Both scripts accept a separate output directory. [Tally Room polling-place workbook](https://drive.google.com/open?id=1Za82Rt_8dsKk1lIQvX1wWTGAwYGpJuhB) · [Source reader](record.py)
